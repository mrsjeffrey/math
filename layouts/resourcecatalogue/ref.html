Strand A: Quadratic Relations (MPM2D)

solve problems involving proportional reasoning A1.1: collect data that can be represented as a quadratic relation (from experiments or secondary sources), graph the data, and draw a curve of best fit (with or without technology); A1.2: determine, through investigation, that a quadratic relation y = ax2 + bx + c (a ≠ 0) can be represented as a parabola and that a table of values yields a constant second difference; A1.3: identify key features of a parabola (axis of symmetry, vertex, y-intercept, zeros, maximum/minimum) and use appropriate terminology; A1.4: compare, through investigation, features of y = x2 and y = 2x, including the meaning of a negative exponent and of zero as an exponent.
overall expectation: Relating the Graph of y = x2 and Its Transformations relate transformations of the graph of y = x2 to the algebraic representation y = a(x − h)2 + k; and specific expectations: A2.1: identify, through investigation, the effect of transformations (translations, reflections in the x-axis, vertical stretches/compressions) on y = x2 by considering parameters a, h, and k; A2.2: explain the roles of a, h, and k in y = a(x − h)2 + k, and identify the vertex and the equation of the axis of symmetry; A2.3: sketch, by hand, the graph of y = a(x − h)2 + k by applying transformations to y = x2 (and verify using technology, where appropriate); A2.4: determine the equation y = a(x − h)2 + k for a given parabola.
overall expectation: Solving Quadratic Equations solve quadratic equations and interpret solutions with respect to the corresponding relations; and specific expectations: A3.1: expand and simplify second-degree polynomial expressions involving one variable, using a variety of tools and strategies; A3.2: factor polynomial expressions (common factors, trinomials, differences of squares), using a variety of tools and strategies; A3.3: determine, through investigation, the connection between factors of a quadratic expression and the x-intercepts (zeros) of the corresponding graph (y = a(x − r)(x − s)); A3.4: interpret real and non-real roots of quadratic equations (using graphing technology), and relate roots to x-intercepts; A3.5: complete the square to write y = ax2 + bx + c in the form y = a(x − h)2 + k in situations involving no fractions; A3.6: sketch or graph a quadratic relation given in the form y = ax2 + bx + c, using a variety of methods (e.g., intercepts and symmetry; completing the square; transformations); A3.7: explore the algebraic development of the quadratic formula (student reproduction of the general derivation not required); A3.8: solve quadratic equations with real roots using a variety of methods (factoring, quadratic formula, graphing).
overall expectation: Solving Problems Involving Quadratic Relations solve problems involving quadratic relations; and specific expectations: A4.1: determine the zeros and the maximum or minimum value of a quadratic relation from its graph or its defining equation; A4.2: solve problems arising from realistic situations represented by a quadratic relation (graph or equation), with and without technology.

Strand B: Analytic Geometry (MPM2D)

overall expectation: Using Linear Systems to Solve Problems model and solve problems involving the intersection of two straight lines; and specific expectations: B1.1: solve systems of two linear equations involving two variables using substitution or elimination (and verify algebraically and graphically); B1.2: solve problems from realistic situations represented by a system of two linear equations, choosing an appropriate algebraic or graphical method.
overall expectation: Solving Problems Involving Properties of Line Segments solve problems using analytic geometry involving properties of lines and line segments; and specific expectations: B2.1: develop and use the midpoint formula to solve problems; B2.2: develop and use the length (distance) formula to solve problems; B2.3: develop the equation of a circle with centre (0, 0) and radius r using the distance formula; B2.4: determine the radius given x2 + y2 = r2, or write the equation given r, and sketch the circle; B2.5: solve problems involving slope, length, and midpoint of a line segment (e.g., right bisector; distance from a point to a line), and verify using dynamic geometry software, where appropriate.
overall expectation: Using Analytic Geometry to Verify Geometric Properties verify geometric properties of triangles and quadrilaterals using analytic geometry; and specific expectations: B3.1: investigate (e.g., using dynamic geometry software or paper folding) characteristics and properties of geometric figures (e.g., medians in a triangle; similar figures on the sides of a right triangle); B3.2: verify, using algebraic techniques and analytic geometry, characteristics of geometric figures (e.g., perpendicular lines; equilateral triangle given coordinates); B3.3: plan and implement a multi-step strategy using analytic geometry and algebra to verify a geometric property (e.g., midsegment theorem; rectangle diagonals bisect).

Strand C: Trigonometry (MPM2D)

overall expectation: Investigating Similarity and Solving Problems Involving Similar Triangles use ratio and proportion to investigate similar triangles and solve problems related to similarity; and specific expectations: C1.1: verify, through investigation, properties of similar triangles (corresponding angles equal; corresponding sides proportional); C1.2: describe and compare similarity and congruence; C1.3: solve problems involving similar triangles in realistic situations (e.g., shadows, reflections, scale models, surveying).
overall expectation: Solving Problems Involving the Trigonometry of Right Triangles solve problems involving right triangles using primary trigonometric ratios and the Pythagorean theorem; and specific expectations: C2.1: determine, through investigation, the relationship between ratios of sides in similar right triangles, and define sine, cosine, and tangent ratios; C2.2: determine measures of sides and angles in right triangles using primary trig ratios and the Pythagorean theorem; C2.3: solve real-life right-triangle problems (e.g., surveying, navigation, height of an inaccessible object), using primary trig ratios and the Pythagorean theorem.
overall expectation: Solving Problems Involving the Trigonometry of Acute Triangles solve problems involving acute triangles using the sine law and the cosine law; and specific expectations: C3.1: explore the development of the sine law within acute triangles (student reproduction of the derivation not required); C3.2: explore the development of the cosine law within acute triangles and identify its relationship to the Pythagorean theorem and the cosine of an angle in a right triangle; C3.3: solve problems involving acute triangles using the sine law and the cosine law.
